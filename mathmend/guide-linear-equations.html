5 common mistakes when solving linear equations

These are some of the most frequent errors in student working, with the correct method and a check you can do yourself.

1. Multiplying only the first term in a bracket

Solve 3(x − 2) = 2x + 5

Wrong: 3x − 2 = 2x + 5 (the 2 was not multiplied by 3)

Right: 3x − 6 = 2x + 5 → x = 11

Check: 3(11 − 2) = 27 and 2(11) + 5 = 27.

2. Moving a term without changing its sign

Solve 5x + 7 = 22

Wrong: 5x = 22 + 7

Right: subtract 7 from both sides: 5x = 15 → x = 3

Check: 5(3) + 7 = 22.

3. Dividing only one term

Solve 4x + 8 = 20

Wrong: x + 8 = 5 (only 4x and 20 were divided by 4)

Right: 4x = 12 → x = 3, or divide every term: x + 2 = 5

Check: 4(3) + 8 = 20.

4. Losing a negative sign in a bracket

Solve −2(x − 4) = 10

Wrong: −2x − 8 = 10

Right: −2x + 8 = 10 → −2x = 2 → x = −1

Check: −2(−1 − 4) = −2(−5) = 10.

5. Clearing a fraction from only one term

Solve x/3 + 2 = 5

Wrong: x + 2 = 15 (only x/3 and 5 were multiplied by 3)

Right: multiply every term by 3: x + 6 = 15 → x = 9

Check: 9/3 + 2 = 5.
